1. Nested Variant Attribute Builder

Description: Allows parent products to house complex, multi-tiered variations under a single dashboard.

How it works: Instead of manually inputting 50 separate forms for 50 variations of a shoe, a user creates one parent product ("Air Max 90") and inputs options (5 colors, 10 sizes). The system instantly expands this matrix, creating all 50 distinct SKU profiles automatically.

purpose: Drastically reduces data entry times from hours to seconds for complex retail catalogs.

Main Functions:

1.Create a parent product with multiple attributes.

2.Automatically generate SKU combinations from selected attributes.

3.Assign unique SKU codes to each variation.

4.Allow users to edit individual variations after generation.

5.Manage stock, price, and other details for each SKU.

Activities:

1.Create a parent product, e.g., Air Max 90.

2.Add attributes such as Color and Size.

3.Enter the available options, e.g., 5 colors and 10 sizes.

4.System automatically generates all 50 possible combinations.

5.System creates a unique SKU profile for each combination.

6.User reviews or edits the generated SKU details.

7.User saves the product and its variations to the inventory.





2. Universal History Log

Description: A complete audit trail tracking changes made to product attributes.

How it works: If a manager updates a product's name, brand definition, or weight metric, the system logs the exact timestamp, the user ID who performed the action, and a "before-and-after" snapshot of the data.

purpose: Essential for internal accountability, identifying who made data errors, and recovering lost information.

Main Functions:
1.Record every change made to product information.

2.Store the date and exact time of each change.

3.Identify the user who made the change.

4.Display before-and-after values.

5.Allow authorized users to review previous changes.

6.Support recovery or correction of incorrect product information.

Activities:
1.A user opens a product record.

2.The user changes an attribute, such as the product name or weight.

3.System automatically records the change.

4.System stores the user's ID and timestamp.

5.System records the before and after values.

6.A manager views the product's history log.

7.The manager identifies who made a particular change and when it occurred.
